Why Does WIP Drop Downstream?

Notice how buffer inventory descends monotonically from b1 (3.48) down to b9 (1.59) despite equal service rates (μ = 1.0) and equal capacities (bm = 5).

The Upstream Mechanism: Machine 1 has infinite raw materials at its entrance and is never starved. When a downstream disruption occurs, parts rapidly pile up in the front buffers.

The Downstream Mechanism: Machine 10 has infinite room to discharge finished items and is never blocked. When starved of parts, it drains its preceding buffers to 0 almost instantaneously.

Total Queue WIP = 3.48 + 3.11 + ... + 1.59 = 22.91 workpieces

Buffer Allocation (+5 Spaces)

"The company wants to allocate 5 more buffer spaces. Make a suggestion where these buffer spaces should be allocated and provide the reasoning for your suggestion."

Academic Solution: Allocate the 5 additional spaces in the center of the line (buffers b4, b5, and b6), forming a bowl-shaped distribution (e.g., capacities 5-5-6-7-7-6-5-5-4).

Theoretical Rationale (The Bowl Phenomenon): Starvation waves propagate downstream while blocking waves propagate upstream. Center buffers act as the line's central damper, shielding the upstream half from downstream stalls and vice versa. Placing buffers at the extremities causes asymmetric waste (excess WIP at the front, persistent starvation at the back).

b

General Rules of Thumb for Buffer Allocation

"Discuss general rules of thumb for the buffer allocation problem."

  • The Bowl Shape: For balanced flow lines with identical stations, buffers should be allocated symmetrically, with maximum capacity in the center.
  • Bottleneck Protection: If a station is slower (lower bottleneck rate rb / higher mean processing time te), allocate buffers directly before it (to prevent starvation) and directly after it (to prevent blocking).
  • Variability Absorption (MTTF & MTTR): Stations with higher breakdown frequencies (lower MTTF), longer repair times (higher MTTR), or higher squared coefficients of variation (cv2) require larger adjacent buffers.
  • Diminishing Marginal Returns: Buffer allocation exhibits strict concavity. Adding buffer capacity beyond a certain threshold drastically inflates WIP with negligible throughput gain.

Methodology & Simulation Disclaimer

Stochastic Engine

Processing times are generated using inverse transform sampling: T = -ln(1 - U) / μ operating under the standard Blocking-After-Service (BAS) protocol.

Steady-State Definition

A production line enters steady state when the probability distribution of station states and buffer occupancies becomes stationary and invariant over time. Use Steady State to bypass the initial transient warm-up phase.

Sample Variance

The targets (E[TH] = 0.764, E[Lq] = 22.91) represent asymptotic theoretical steady-state metrics derived from analytical queueing decompositions. Live browser Monte Carlo runs naturally fluctuate around these figures due to finite sample variance before complete convergence.

Variability & Classes

The exercise assumes memoryless exponential service times (μ = 1.0, cvs2 = 1.0), representing Moderate Variability (MV). In industrial practice, raw times are often Low Variability (LV, cv < 0.75), while effective times include outages governed by MTTF and MTTR.
