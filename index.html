Why Does WIP Drop Downstream?

Notice how buffer inventory descends monotonically from B1 (3.48) down to B9 (1.59) despite equal service rates (μ = 1.0) and equal capacities (C = 5).

The Upstream Mechanism: Machine 1 has infinite raw materials at its entrance and is never starved. When a downstream disruption occurs, parts rapidly pile up in the front buffers.

The Downstream Mechanism: Machine 10 has infinite room to discharge finished items and is never blocked. When starved of parts, it drains its preceding buffers to 0 almost instantaneously.

Total Queue WIP = 3.48 + 3.11 + ... + 1.59 = 22.91 workpieces

Buffer Allocation (+5 Spaces)

"The company wants to allocate 5 more buffer spaces. Make a suggestion where these buffer spaces should be allocated and provide the reasoning for your suggestion."

Academic Solution: Allocate the 5 additional spaces in the center of the line (buffers B4, B5, and B6), forming a bowl-shaped distribution (e.g., capacities 5-5-6-7-7-6-5-5-4).

Theoretical Rationale (The Bowl Phenomenon): Starvation waves propagate downstream while blocking waves propagate upstream. Center buffers act as the line's central damper, shielding the upstream half from downstream stalls and vice versa. Placing buffers at the extremities causes asymmetric waste (excess WIP at the front, persistent starvation at the back).

b

General Rules of Thumb for Buffer Allocation

"Discuss general rules of thumb for the buffer allocation problem."

  • The Bowl Shape: For balanced flow lines with identical stations, buffers should be allocated symmetrically, with maximum capacity in the center.
  • Bottleneck Protection: If a station is slower (mean processing time te is larger), allocate buffers directly before it (to prevent starvation) and directly after it (to prevent blocking).
  • Variability Absorption (MTTF & MTTR): Stations with higher breakdown frequencies (lower MTTF), longer outage durations (higher MTTR), or higher natural process variance (ce2) require larger buffers to decouple the surrounding line.
  • Diminishing Returns: Buffer allocation exhibits strict concavity. Adding buffer capacity beyond a certain threshold drastically increases WIP with negligible throughput gain.

Methodology & Simulation Disclaimer

Stochastic Engine

Line dynamics and buffer occupancy are modeled via continuous discrete-event Monte Carlo simulation. Effective processing times are generated using inverse transform sampling: T = -ln(1 - U) / μ operating under the standard Blocking-After-Service (BAS) protocol.

Sample Variance vs. Benchmark

The exercise benchmark targets (E[TH] = 0.764, E[WIPq] = 22.91) represent asymptotic steady-state solutions derived from analytical queueing decompositions. Live browser simulations converge toward these values, but exhibit natural stochastic noise over finite runs. Use Steady State to jump straight to convergence.

Industrial Distribution Context

The exercise assumes memoryless exponential service times (μ = 1.0, ce = 1.0) for mathematical tractability. Real-world manufacturing assembly stations usually display lower natural cycle-time variance (ce ≈ 0.2–0.5), with variability instead dominated by discrete machine outages modeled via MTTF and MTTR.
